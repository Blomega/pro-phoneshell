Plain PPO ties Gemini-3.1-Pro on Apple's own iOS benchmark; Apple's proposed GiGPPO method scores lower getting there

Apple's new online reinforcement-learning framework for training UI-navigation agents directly on live iOS apps (arXiv:2610.39264, submitted 29 October 2026) reports its 8B agent "tying with Gemini-3.1-Pro on real iOS applications" and gaining "nearly a sevenfold improvement" over its supervised baseline. Both numbers, in the paper's own Table 1, belong to plain PPO at training step 336. GiGPPO, the neighbor-conditioned method the paper actually proposes, scores lower than plain PPO on both of those benchmarks at the same step, and only pulls ahead on iOSWorld, the one benchmark where every method in the table scores worst.

The paper, "Online Dense Reinforcement Learning with Planning-Grounding Agent for UI Navigation on Live iOS Apps," comes from five Apple researchers: Peitong Duan, Khashayar Gatmiry, Jeffrey Nichols, Samy Bengio, and Emmanuel Abbe. It trains an 8B agent, built from a Planning agent and a UI Grounding model, with Proximal Policy Optimization against hundreds of concurrent virtual iOS devices, arguing that supervised fine-tuning and offline RL "often memorize static trajectories, leading to brittle execution and an inability to recover from errors." The paper's headline contribution is GiGPPO, a neighbor-conditioned PPO variant meant to stabilize the critic using step-level LLM-as-Judge rewards instead of rewarding only the final outcome.

Table 1: the headline numbers are PPO's, not GiGPPO's

Section 4.3's Table 1 scores eleven training checkpoints (three reward designs across three step counts) plus five reference models on three benchmarks: UINavBench (116 tasks, 36 real iOS apps), iOSWorld (133 tasks, 26 synthetic iOS apps), and AndroidWorld (116 tasks, 20 real Android apps, used as the out-of-distribution generalization test). The paper's own prose credits "PPO Step 336 (2 epochs)" with tying Gemini-3.1-Pro at 32.4% on UINavBench and with "scoring nearly 7 times higher (32.8% vs. 4.7%)" than the SFT baseline on AndroidWorld. GiGPPO at the same step scores 28.4% on UINavBench and 31.9% on AndroidWorld, below plain PPO on both. GiGPPO's one clear win over PPO is on iOSWorld: 9.8% against PPO's 2.3%, both still far under Gemini-3.1-Pro's 39.5% there and under the SFT baseline's own 4.5%, which PPO at step 336 actually falls below.

Where GiGPPO's reward design actually pays off

GiGPPO's difference from plain PPO is in what it rewards and when: PPO as ablated here comes in a terminal-only variant (reward only at episode end) and a step-level variant, while GiGPPO adds neighbor-conditioned value estimation on top of step-level LLM-as-Judge rewards, meant to reduce critic variance when an 8B policy is being pushed by a much larger judge model's feedback at every step rather than only at the end. That extra stabilization is exactly the kind of thing that should matter most on the noisiest environment, and iOSWorld, built from synthetic rather than real iOS apps, is where every single model and checkpoint in Table 1 scores worst: the SFT baseline sits at 4.5%, the strongest reference model (Gemini-3.1-Pro) tops out at 39.5%, nothing like its 60.8% on AndroidWorld. GiGPPO's 9.8% there is still a small absolute number, but it is more than four times PPO's 2.3% on the same benchmark, and the only place in the table where the paper's proposed method is unambiguously ahead of its own ablation.

What it means for reading an RL-for-UI result

An ablation table that includes the method actually proposed, next to a simpler baseline the authors also ran, is useful exactly because it lets a reader check whether the fancier design earns its complexity. Here it does not, on two of three benchmarks, at the step the paper's own text chooses to highlight. That is not evidence GiGPPO is a bad idea: its neighbor-conditioned critic is explicitly a variance-reduction technique, and iOSWorld, the one noisy environment where everyone's numbers are worst, is exactly where variance reduction should show up first. But a reader who only reads the abstract would come away thinking the paper's named contribution produced the sevenfold AndroidWorld gain and the UINavBench tie with Gemini-3.1-Pro. Table 1 says plain PPO produced both, and GiGPPO trails PPO by 4.0 points on UINavBench and 0.9 points on AndroidWorld to get its 7.5-point lead on iOSWorld. Whether that trade is worth it depends entirely on which benchmark a reader cares about, a judgment the abstract does not offer and the reader has to make from the table.

What would prove this wrong

We claim Table 1 of arXiv:2610.39264v1 reports PPO at Step 336 scoring 32.4% on UINavBench and 32.8% on AndroidWorld, GiGPPO at Step 336 scoring 28.4% and 31.9% on the same two benchmarks respectively, and GiGPPO scoring 9.8% against PPO's 2.3% on iOSWorld, all checkable directly against the cited arXiv v1 HTML. This should be revisited if a later revision of the paper reports a different ablation ordering, publishes a training-step budget where GiGPPO leads PPO on UINavBench or AndroidWorld as well, or if a follow-up paper reproduces both methods at larger scale and finds the current ordering was a step-336 artifact, by 30 June 2027.
