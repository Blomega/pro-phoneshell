Tactile's four-agent accessibility gains are a best-of-three upper bound, and one of the three is the baseline

The cross-agent result in Tactile's abstract ("consistent gains across Codex, Claude Code, OpenCode, and Goose" on 96 macOS tasks, arXiv:2607.14443, submitted 16 July 2026) is defined in Section 5 as "a skill-optional upper bound computed from the best per-task result among no-skill, tactile-implicit, and tactile-explicit settings." A maximum over three settings that include the baseline cannot score below the baseline. For Goose the gain is 41.67% to 43.75%, which is 2 tasks.

"Tactile: Giving Computer-Using Agents Hands and Feet" is by Yong Liu, Zhenyi Zhong and Zhanpeng Shi of Shanghai Jiao Tong University. Tactile is an MCP server with a Swift runtime that handles macOS Accessibility traversal and actions, ScreenCapture, Vision OCR and input events, exposing eight tools such as get_app_state, click and perform_secondary_action. Its design argument is one we agree with: a coordinate click "collapses target grounding, action execution, and outcome verification into a single ambiguous operation," and an agent should prefer a named, verifiable action when the operating system offers one. This note is about how much of that the evaluation shows.

The paper has no results table; the numbers are in two paragraphs of Section 5.1

The HTML version contains two tables, one of runtime requirements and one of application examples, and neither holds a score. The metric is "Success@100, the percentage of tasks completed within 100 interaction steps." The first comparison is Codex against Codex with Tactile on "currently graded macOSWorld samples": 41.06% to 50.00% overall, 45.22% to 55.26% on AX-adapted tasks, 27.78% to 33.33% on Limited-AX tasks. The number of tasks in that sample and in each split is not reported. The second comparison is the 96-task subset across four agent clients.

Source: "Tactile: Giving Computer-Using Agents Hands and Feet," arXiv:2607.14443v1, Section 5.1, submitted 16 July 2026, read 10 November 2026. The 96-task "With Tactile" column is the paper's skill-optional upper bound. Task counts are our arithmetic from the percentages.

On 96 tasks one task is 1.04 points, so the percentages convert cleanly: Codex gains 11 tasks, Claude Code 10, OpenCode 7, Goose 2. The paper adds that on Limited-AX tasks "Goose shows no change." No run counts, seeds or intervals are given, and Section 5.3 lists "report uncertainty estimates" as future work. A 2-task difference on 96 is inside any plausible run-to-run noise; an 11-task difference may not be, but the paper gives no way to tell.

An oracle over three settings measures headroom, which is a weaker claim than a gain

Section 5 states the construction and Section 5.3 repeats it: "The skill-optional cross-agent score is an upper bound rather than a learned routing policy." For each task the With Tactile score takes whichever of three runs succeeded: the agent with no skill, the agent with Tactile available implicitly, the agent told explicitly to use it. Two consequences follow. The score counts a task as solved "with Tactile" when only the no-skill run solved it. And three attempts beat one attempt on any task with run-to-run variance, with or without a new tool. The abstract carries the cross-agent claim and omits the caveat. Whether the Codex-only 41.06% to 50.00% comparison is a single configuration or the same oracle is not stated clearly enough for us to say.

The ladder itself is where the interesting measurement would be. Tactile defines three levels (accessibility semantics, OCR-grounded coordinates, visual fallback) and three observation modes (ax, ax_ocr, ax_ocr_visual). The paper reports no count of how often each level fired or how tasks fared when Level 3 was needed; the AX-adapted and Limited-AX labels are assigned per task in advance. It gives no numeric trigger for escalation, and Section 7 says future work should record "why structured evidence was insufficient." The baseline is a "no-skill" agent client, and Section 5.3 names a "stronger visual-only baseline" as still needed, so the evaluation does not compare accessibility against screenshots.

What it means for the accessibility-versus-vision decision

The direction of the Codex split is what the design predicts: a 10.04-point gain where the tree is usable and 5.55 where it is thin. That is one agent, one unreported sample size, no interval. Treat it as an anecdote that agrees with the hypothesis. The four-agent claim should be read as "there exist tasks where some Tactile setting succeeded and no-skill failed": 11, 10, 7 and 2 of them at most, minus however many the no-skill run would also have picked up on a second and third try.

What would turn this into evidence is cheap to state: a fixed Tactile configuration per agent, three runs of each arm, and a per-step log of which ladder level acted. The trace format already exists; the paper shows one Zoom task with eleven state snapshots and traversal times of 0.24s to 0.85s.

phoneshell has the same ladder on iOS and a measurement of its bottom rung. The tree is read first; phoneshell/agent/observation.py overlays numbered boxes on the screenshot only on stated conditions, two of them in its own words: "the accessibility tree is very thin" or "the tree exposes nothing interactive." Targets recovered from pixels by phoneshell/perception/blind.py are tagged source="pixel" with a confidence, and a pixel candidate that overlaps something the tree named is dropped. The house result from 120 paired runs on a physical iPhone 17 Pro Max is the number Tactile lacks: when the answer exists only as pixels, success is 100% with the screenshot and 0% without it (p < 0.0001), and when the answer is in the tree the image changes nothing while adding about 35% to per-step cost. That is a statement about tasks. Tactile's 5.55-point Limited-AX figure is a statement about a task label, and the two should not be compared directly.

What would prove this wrong

We claim arXiv:2607.14443v1 defines its cross-agent With Tactile score as a best-per-task result among three settings that include no-skill, and reports Goose at 41.67% and 43.75% on 96 tasks. Our prediction: if a revision or follow-up published by 30 June 2027 reports a single fixed Tactile configuration with at least three runs per arm, the Goose gain will not be statistically distinguishable from zero and the Codex gain on the 96-task subset will be smaller than 11.46 points. A fixed-configuration Codex gain of 11.46 points or more with a 95% interval excluding zero would show the oracle construction was not inflating the result.
