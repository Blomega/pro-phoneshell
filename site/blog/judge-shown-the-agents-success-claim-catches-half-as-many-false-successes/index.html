Show an LLM judge the agent's own success claim and its recall on false successes drops from 0.49 to 0.23

Appending a GUI agent's own final report to the input of an LLM completion judge cut that judge's recall on false success claims from 0.49 to 0.23 with Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, across 465 annotated Android runs (arXiv:2610.29609, submitted 21 October 2026, accepted to ICSE 2027). Measured on the trace verdict alone, before any claim gating, the gap is 43.4 points. Of the 80 verdicts that changed when the report was shown, 79 were harmful flips and 1 was a recovery.

The paper is "Who Verifies the Agent's Success Claim? Self-Report Leakage in GUI-Agent Completion Judging" by Yunfeng Zhu and Zhenyu Chen (Nanjing University), Shengcheng Yu (Technical University of Munich) and Zihan Li (Nanjing Normal University). It starts from 1,384 in-scope execution attempts by two agents, AppAgent and Mobile-Agent-v3, on AndroidWorld, an AndroidWorld extension and Themis. After clean passes and non-auditable runs are removed, annotators labelled 465 records. 180 of them are what the authors call a dangerous misreport: "an unhedged success claim that the trace does not support as causal accomplishment during the run." Each agent contributes 90.

The same judge reads the same trace twice; only the agent's report differs

Section IV holds everything fixed between two conditions: task, trace, serialization, model, scoring rule, temperature 0, and five runs aggregated by a 3-of-5 vote. In the "aware" condition the agent's final report and per-step self-reports are appended. In the "blind" condition they are withheld. Two hosted judge models are reported separately and never pooled. Gold labels come from two annotators with a Cohen's kappa of 0.79 on the dangerous-misreport label before adjudication.

The direction holds in all four rows. The size does not: DeepSeek-284B-A13B loses 8 points of claim-gated recall on the full set where Qwen3.5-35B-A3B loses 26, and on held-out Batch 2 DeepSeek's 9 harmful flips against 2 recoveries give a sign-test p of 0.065, which the authors call directionally consistent. Batch 2 has 50 positives. Treat the DeepSeek held-out row as suggestive.

Withholding the report helps, and still leaves a judge at F1 0.62

The paper states it flatly: "Report isolation alone is not sufficient." A prompt-blind judge reaches F1 0.62 (Qwen) and 0.64 (DeepSeek) on the 465 records. The authors' structured auditor splits the job into three parts. A claim reader C classifies what the report asserts. A claim-blind extractor E sees only task and trace and fills 5 fields (action, target_id, transition, committed, final_supports_goal); in the paper's words, "the extractor is not allowed to output completed or not_completed." A deterministic checker G maps those fields to a verdict. That design reaches F1 0.75 and 0.78 on all 465 records, and 0.77 and 0.81 on held-out Batch 2. Human annotators reading the trace alone score 0.90 and 0.95, so a gap remains.

Three things in the tables are worth reading against the prose.

First, Section V-B says "matched claim-aware judging trails both isolated methods on recall." Table III does not support that for DeepSeek: on Batch 2 the matched claim-aware judge has recall 0.56 against the prompt-blind judge's 0.52, and on the full set the two tie at 0.52. The sentence is true for Qwen and for the structured auditor everywhere.

Second, the abstract's F1 of 0.75 to 0.78 is computed on all 465 records, 335 of which (Batch 1) were used to develop the auditor. The held-out figures are 0.77 with a 95% interval of [0.67, 0.86] for Qwen and 0.81 with [0.72, 0.88] for DeepSeek. The abstract reports both, but only the Batch 2 numbers are out of sample.

Third, a rule with no model in it, flagging every success claim in the audit set, scores F1 0.57. The matched claim-aware Qwen judge scores 0.56 and the DeepSeek one 0.63. For Qwen, five LLM calls per record and a majority vote bought nothing over the rule.

What it means if your benchmark is graded by a model

Any pipeline that hands a judge the agent's last message is running the aware condition. That includes most LLM-as-judge setups for mobile agents, because the final message is the cheapest summary of a trace whose median length here is about 89K characters. The cost of that convenience is now measured: 79 lost detections against 1 gained for one judge model, 31 against 7 for the other.

The paper's deployment numbers give the price of fixing it. With flag and review both blocking an automatic pass, the auditor stops 138 of 180 dangerous misreports (Qwen) and 159 of 180 (DeepSeek) while sending 48% and 56% of the audit set to inspection. Against all 1,384 attempts the authors put that load at roughly 16% and 19%. Between 12% and 23% of false successes still pass silently.

phoneshell sits at the far end of this axis because it has no judge to persuade. run_check in phoneshell/bench/verify.py accepts the agent's words as a said argument, and exactly one of its 12 check kinds, answer_contains, reads it; the other 11 query the phone. A grep of the 80 public task files finds 153 checks, 20 of them answer_contains, each a substring match against ground truth fixed in advance for tasks whose deliverable is information. Our judgement: that removes leakage and keeps a different weakness, since a substring match can pass an answer that happens to contain the right word. We covered the general failure in agents report success, the device disagrees.

What would prove this wrong

We claim Table II of arXiv:2610.29609v1 reports claim-gated recall of 0.23 aware and 0.49 blind for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, 0.44 and 0.52 for DeepSeek-284B-A13B, and harmful-to-recovery flip counts of 79 to 1 and 31 to 7. Our prediction: any paper published by 30 June 2027 that repeats an aware/blind ablation on a mobile or desktop agent benchmark with at least 100 false-success positives will find blind recall at or above aware recall for every judge model it tests. One judge model with significantly higher aware recall at that sample size proves the prediction wrong.
