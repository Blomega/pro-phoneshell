Coati's completion audit moves false done from 9 of 112 to 16 of 299, a gap the paper does not test

Coati's abstract (arXiv:2611.02644, submitted 2 November 2026) says "VerifyLoop cuts false completion claims by about a third at comparable success." Table 4 shows what is being compared: 9 false of 112 declared completions without the audit, in one trial, against 16 false of 299 accepted completions with it, pooled over three trials. That is 8.0% against 5.4%. A two-proportion test on those counts gives z = 1.01 and p = 0.31 (Fisher exact p = 0.35).

"Coati: Host-Grounded Execution for Installable Mobile GUI Agents" is by Yudou Ying, Pu Shi, Hanlei Shi and Yuhua Zheng (Hangzhou Institute for Advanced Study, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, and one independent researcher). Coati is an Android app that drives other apps through AccessibilityService. On all 116 AndroidWorld tasks, with Gemini 3.8 Flash as the backbone of every agent, it reports 86.5% success over three trials against 77.6% for Artemis (GUI). The baselines were re-run by the authors through one runner, in an Android 13 emulator with a Pixel 6 profile. This note is about the reliability claim, which is the part closest to our own work.

Table 4 sets one trial against three, and the success rates differ by 3.2 points

The paper defines false done as the "share of declared completions that the evaluator judged failed." The evaluator is AndroidWorld's own, which the authors describe as one "that inspects the resulting app state rather than the agent's claim." So the metric is a device-graded false-success rate. Table 3 also reports it for two re-run baselines: 7.5% for Midscene.js and 14.5% for FinalRun, each from one trial. Artemis does not report one.

The difference between the two Coati rows is 2.7 points. Its 95% interval from the reported counts runs from -3.0 to 8.3 points, so it includes zero and it includes a reduction twice the size claimed. The paper reports no interval and no test. It also reports no per-trial false done count for C2, so the one like-for-like comparison available, C1 against C2's first trial (both at 89.7% success), cannot be made. This calculation treats each completion claim as independent, which pooled trials of the same 116 tasks are not; correcting for that widens the interval further.

On success the paper says C1 "reaches 89.7% in its single trial and C2 reaches the same 89.7% in its matching first trial," and attributes the 86.5% mean to "trial-to-trial variance rather than a cost of the audit." That may be right. The three C2 trials span 89.7 to 82.8, a sample standard deviation of 3.5, and C1 was run once. One trial cannot show the audit is free.

The audit is a model reading screens, and its two failure cases are in the paper

When a route claims completion, a Fast model compares the current screen, the last result screen from every other app the task used, and any delegated-loop record against the values in the task. Across three trials it rejected 61 claims. Of those tasks, 40 were then solved and 21 ended as reported failures. It accepted 299 claims, 16 of them false, which leaves 283 that the state evaluator agreed with. The missing cell is how many of the 61 rejections hit a result that was already correct. Rejecting correct work costs steps under a fixed budget, and the medium and hard rows of Table 4 are where C2 trails C1 (79.6 against 86.1, and 68.4 against 73.7).

The authors give one example of each error. A routed run of "hide every listing marked Sold" hid one of six, claimed completion, and the audit of the final screen accepted it because the other five were below the fold. In the other direction, a run that had changed the app state correctly "was rejected three times because no single final screen shows all six items." Their own summary: "an honest auditor must either accept unseen results or reject correct ones." There is a third case. In an earlier trial "two of 116 completion audits timed out and the tasks were reported as done"; the current build reports those as unverified.

What it means if you are adding a completion check to an agent

A 5.4% false done rate with the audit on is the number to carry away, with its caveat: one backbone, one emulator, three trials. It says that an agent-side audit built from screens still lets roughly one accepted completion in nineteen through wrong, on a benchmark whose grader reads app state. The 8.0% without it is one trial. Section 6 of the paper lists "a limited number of runs" among its limits.

The useful design detail is the third status. An audit that cannot run produces "unverified", which is neither success nor failure. We make the same separation one level up: phoneshell reports a task that was never attempted as not scored. Coati applies it inside the agent, where a timeout would otherwise turn into a success claim.

phoneshell has no in-agent completion audit. The agent's claim is never an input to the grade, except for the one answer_contains check on information tasks. phoneshell/bench/verify.py opens with the rule: "Every check reads the phone directly rather than believing the agent's account of itself." That makes false done cheap for us to report and we have: in an earlier note, 16 of 27 failures on a physical iPhone were reported as successes by the model that failed them. What Coati adds is a measured before and after for an agent-side gate, on a public benchmark, with the counts printed. The counts are what let anyone check the claim, and they show it needs more trials.

Check it yourself

# Table 3, Table 4 and Section 4.3
open "https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02644v1"

# The test on the reported counts: 9 of 112 against 16 of 299
python3 -c "
import math
a,n1,b,n2=9,112,16,299
p1,p2=a/n1,b/n2; pp=(a+b)/(n1+n2)
z=(p1-p2)/math.sqrt(pp*(1-pp)*(1/n1+1/n2))
se=math.sqrt(p1*(1-p1)/n1+p2*(1-p2)/n2)
print(round(p1*100,1),round(p2*100,1),round(z,2),round(math.erfc(abs(z)/math.sqrt(2)),2))
print(round((p1-p2-1.96*se)*100,1),round((p1-p2+1.96*se)*100,1))"
# 8.0 5.4 1.01 0.31
# -3.0 8.3

# phoneshell: the grader never reads the agent's claim, with one named exception
grep -n "rather than believing the agent's account" phoneshell/bench/verify.py
grep -n "The ONLY check that grades what the model said" phoneshell/bench/verify.py

What would prove this wrong

We claim that arXiv:2611.02644v1 supports its "about a third" reduction with 9 of 112 against 16 of 299 and that those counts do not separate the two rates. Our prediction: if a revision or follow-up published by 30 June 2027 reports false done for C1 and C2 with at least three trials each, the relative reduction will be smaller than one third or its 95% interval will include zero. A matched three-trial comparison showing a reduction of one third or more with an interval excluding zero would show the audit does what the abstract says.
